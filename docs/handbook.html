Threadback · handbook · September 2026

A chain-of-custody ledger for recycled cotton, from cutting waste to garment

Threadback is a shared record of what happened to recycled material, written by the companies that handled it: waste trader, recycler, spinner, fabric mill and garment manufacturer. For every recycled content claim it answers three questions.

OriginWhere did the material come from? Every kg traced to a named factory.
DocumentationWho handled it, and do their documents agree at every handoff?
Mass balanceDo the kilograms add up from waste to garment, without double claiming?

Niche for the first version: pre-consumer cotton cutting waste (jhut) in Bangladesh, mechanically recycled, spun at 40% recycled content, knitted into single jersey and sewn into T-shirts for EU buyers. GRS only. Knitted fabric only.

Business

Who buys it

There are two customers, and they are different people. The user is the Bangladeshi garment exporter, which does the daily work of assembling evidence. The payer at scale is usually the EU brand, which makes the claim to consumers and carries the legal risk. Every interview so far was in Bangladesh; the EU side is untested.

Recommendation: pilot with Bangladeshi manufacturers, and use the pilot to test whether their EU buyers pay

Start where your access and evidence are: one or two exporters making recycled-cotton products for EU buyers, each bringing its recycler and waste traders. Their deliverable is the evidence pack for a claim: every TC, document, declaration, ledger line and check behind a shipment, ready for the buyer and the certification body. In every pilot, ask for an introduction to the EU buyer of that order and show them the pack and the live chain.

EU rules behind the urgency

  • Directive (EU) 2024/825 on empowering consumers applies from 27 September 2026: no unsubstantiated environmental claims, and sustainability labels only from certification schemes.
  • Ecodesign Regulation (EU) 2024/1781: textiles are a priority group; a Digital Product Passport is expected within a few years.
  • Textile producer responsibility under the revised Waste Framework Directive: fees per product, likely rewarding proven recycled content.

Check current status before quoting any of these in a pitch.

Ask at least five EU brands

  1. What evidence do you get today for "40% recycled cotton"? Who checks it?
  2. Has a claim or shipment been held for missing upstream evidence?
  3. Which tools do you use, and what do they miss before the spinner?
  4. How are you preparing for the 2024/825 claim rules?
  5. Would you pay for a verified chain to the waste source: per supplier, per claim, per year? Whose budget?
  6. Would you require, or pay for, your suppliers to use it?

Decide after the pilot

  • EU buyers ask for access and pay: brand pays; suppliers use it free.
  • Buyers value it but won't pay, and exporters win orders with it: manufacturer pays a low per-factory fee; the evidence pack is the product.
  • Neither pays: test certification bodies as buyers (faster audits from clean data).

Concept

How it works

  1. 1Each company enters what it didThe waste trader declares each batch on a phone, offline if needed. The recycler books goods-in and countersigns. Spinner, mill and manufacturer record production and documents.
  2. 2Every entry is a line in the ledgerLines are only added, never changed. Each line's fingerprint includes the previous one, so editing history is detectable.
  3. 3The ledger becomes a live material flowKg in, out, lost and in stock at every tier, and a mass-balance account per company, updated on every screen as lines land.
  4. 4Checks decide the claimA failing check becomes a gap for the company that owns the data and closes itself when fixed. The claim goes out only when every check passes.

Suppliers join by invitation from their buyer, and each tier invites the next one up. Each company sees its own records and its direct partners. The manufacturer sees the chain. Nobody sees prices.

Using it

User guides

Garment manufacturer customer

  1. Invite your fabric mill; each supplier invites the next one up.
  2. Watch Material flow fill in live; open Chain for the handoffs behind a claim.
  3. Raise gaps on failing checks; they close when the supplier fixes the data.
  4. Record cutting: pieces, fabric per piece, garment weight.
  5. Add a product sheet, add your buyer, create the claim.
  6. When it shows Ready, send the evidence pack: share a read-only link with the buyer, or download and print it for the certification body.

Waste trader phone · offline

  1. Tap New batch when bagging waste for sale.
  2. Batch: weight, bags, slip number, photo of the slip.
  3. Sources: one card per factory with kg and date; the total must match.
  4. Sort: colour, fibre, no elastane, no prints.
  5. Sign with a finger or photograph the paper form. Offline, it is sent later.

Recycler first certified tier

  1. Open the goods-in queue of declared batches.
  2. Record gate weight, moisture, PO and the trader's invoice; countersign if it matches.
  3. Record sorting and shredding: waste in, fibre out, rejects.
  4. Ship fibre to the spinner with TC and documents.

Spinner and fabric mill

  1. Check My tasks for requests and failing checks.
  2. Record production: inputs per receipt (plus virgin cotton at the spinner) and output.
  3. Record shipments with TC, PO, invoice and net packing list.
  4. Fix red cells in a shipment's document cross-check.

Operations

Hosting and data

Recommendation: an EU cloud region (Frankfurt or Ireland) for the pilot

The data is mostly business data; the personal data is small (names, emails, signatures). An EU region meets EU buyers' GDPR expectations from day one. Latency from Bangladesh is fine for this app, and the offline-first design covers weak connections. The move is reversible: the whole state is one ledger in one database.

Before the pilot, ask a Bangladeshi lawyer whether the country's data protection law, still being finalised as of my knowledge, requires any data to stay in Bangladesh. If EU brands turn out not to be part of the model, Singapore or Mumbai are equally good.

Technical

How it is built

Server

  • Node 22.5+, zero dependencies, SQLite built in
  • Accounts, sessions, invitation links, password change
  • Login throttling, 30-day sessions, security headers
  • Visibility enforced on the server
  • Live updates by Server-Sent Events

Ledger

  • Every write is a command, run on the phone at once and re-checked on the server
  • Each accepted command appends one hash-linked line
  • State is the replay of the lines
  • Command ids make phone retries safe

Offline

  • App files cached on the phone
  • Changes wait in an outbox, sent in order on reconnect
  • Changes the server refuses are shown, never dropped
